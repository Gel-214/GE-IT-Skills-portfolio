#### 1. AI-Generated Summary Audit
•	The manual human audit of the AI-generated summaries reveals critical empirical discrepancies across several regional development papers. When evaluating the Mindanao Trust Fund Reconstruction and Development Program Final Evaluation Report (MinDA, 2024), the AI engine misattributed a 14.5% poverty reduction in BARMM solely to agricultural logistics interventions. A verification against official Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data shows that this decline was actually a multi-year trajectory driven by a complex mix of expanded social transfers (4Ps), regional stabilization, and base-effects recovery. 
•	A more severe geographical and physical hallucination occurred regarding the ADB (2022) Improving Growth Corridors in Mindanao Road Sector Project, where the AI claimed a 240km Tawi-Tawi bridge network was 92% complete. Vetting this against the Asian Development Bank (ADB) Project Administration Manual and Q4 2024 progress reports confirms the project actually targets the Zamboanga Peninsula core sub-corridors and the Guicam bridge, with physical progress tracking closer to ~68% due to procurement delays. 
•	Analytical inflation was also detected in the summary of Digal, L. N., et al. (2023) on high-value crops, where the AI reported an average post-harvest loss exceeding 42% for smallholder banana cooperatives in Davao del Norte. Cross-referencing with primary author baseline surveys from the University of the Philippines Mindanao (UPMin) reveals that the 42% figure is an upper-bound outlier for unorganized municipal subsistence farming; organized cooperatives actually experience manageable losses between 12% and 18% caused by black sigatoka and transport bruising rather than a lack of cold chains. 
•	Furthermore, when summarizing the Davao Regional Development Plan 2023-2028 (NEDA XI, 2023), the AI exhibited analytical bias by claiming regional GRDP growth hit 8.1% in 2023 due to digital services and BPO expansions. Vetted against NEDA Region XI official performance indicators and PSA regional accounts, the actual real growth rate was 6.7% (with 8.1% being an optimistic target ceiling), and the true macroeconomic drivers were overwhelmingly Wholesale and Retail Trade, Motor Vehicle Repair, and Construction. 
•	The only fully accurate synthesis was found in the summary of the World Bank (2021) Philippine Rural Development Project (PRDP), which claimed a transport cost reduction of exactly Php 25 per kilogram for cacao farmers in Davao de Oro. This metric was successfully verified by the World Bank and the Department of Agriculture (DA) in the 14th Implementation Support Mission Aide-Memoire, which confirmed a real-world hauling cost reduction of Php 22 to Php 27 per kilogram for agrarian reform communities post-corridor rehabilitation.

#### 2. Critical Reflection on Tool Limitations
Artificial Intelligence, or AI, has made our lives easier with just one prompt away. Tasks were completed easily in one generation, which is already close to perfection. However, this tool also has limitations that most of us humans can do, but AI cannot.

In this assigned task, I have noticed that a single prompt is simply not enough to get the product you want AI to generate; it needs more and more follow-up prompts to achieve the output we want from AI. Moreover, as someone who wants the output to be perfect, I spend most of my patience and thought composing the right prompt to achieve the result I want. 

AI is not perfect. I later found out that most sources ChatGPT provides are either not verified, the study is nowhere to be found on the internet, or simply do not exist. The tools I used to create this task are both ChatGPT and Gemini, which produced different outputs even when I used the same prompt. ChatGPT only gave me a Word document of the table of the vetted literature matrix and critique log, whereas Gemini gave me a complete document. This contrast between the two AI tools led me to choose the output Gemini gave me solely because it was what I wanted. When I verified the sources it gave me, they were found on the internet and exist. 

Through these limitations of the tools I used, I have also come to the realization that, on my part, I have not been good with prompting, or so what they call, I lack the skill of “smart prompting,” which caused the follow-up prompts that I have done. But even so, there are still things that AI cannot perfect, which is why we must be smart about using these tools. We also must be thorough in researching to check whether what AI gives is credible, existing, and true, as well as we must be careful about the data we feed into them.
